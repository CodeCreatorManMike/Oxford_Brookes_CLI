# Assessment, Marking Scale, Progression, Classification, and Extenuating Circumstances
Researched: 2026-09-15
Sources: brookes.ac.uk/regulations/assessment-and-progression/ (marks-and-grades, progression-requirements, failure-and-reassessment, exceptional-circumstances), brookes.ac.uk/regulations/completion/award-classification, brookes.ac.uk/students/your-studies/resits-and-retakes, brookes.ac.uk/students/your-studies/exceptional-circumstances (+ how-to-apply subpage)

## Marking scale (Levels 4-6, i.e. undergraduate years 1-3)
Nine-point scale, pass mark 40% (Grade D):

| % | Grade | Grade Point | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 75-100 | A+ | 4.5 | Pass |
| 70-74 | A | 4.0 | Pass |
| 65-69 | B+ | 3.5 | Pass |
| 60-64 | B | 3.0 | Pass |
| 55-59 | C+ | 2.5 | Pass |
| 50-54 | C | 2.0 | Pass |
| 45-49 | D+ | 1.5 | Pass |
| 40-44 | D | 1.0 | Pass |
| 0-39 | F | 0 | Fail |

(Level 7/postgraduate uses a truncated version with pass mark 50% - not directly relevant to a BSc but noted for completeness.)

## Progression requirements (rules for students starting 2020-21 onward)
- Level 4 -> Level 5: Must complete 120 credits at Level 4; all compulsory modules must be passed; up to 30 Level 4 credits can be passed via "compensation" (condoned marginal fail) to allow progression; max 30 credits can be "trailed" (carried, still to be resolved) into Level 5.
- Level 5 -> Level 6: Must complete 120 credits at Level 5; all compulsory modules passed; up to 15 Level 5 credits compensable; max 15 credits trailed into Level 6.
- Final award: minimum 360 total credits for a Bachelor's with Honours; 120 credits at Level 6 must be passed outright - no compensation allowed at final level.

## Resits and retakes
- One resit permitted per failed assessment (unless professional-body accreditation rules say otherwise); if the resit also fails, one retake opportunity follows (module taken again in full), itself carrying its own resit chance.
- Resit marks are capped at the pass mark - 40% for Levels 4-6 modules (50% at Level 7) - regardless of the mark actually achieved, UNLESS an approved Exceptional Circumstances claim removes the cap for that attempt.
- You cannot resit an assessment you've already passed.
- Resit fees for 2026/27 (undergraduate, per 15-credit module): roughly £720-£2,465 depending on study mode/fee band; fee waivers available for students with household income under £25k, care leavers, estranged students, and certain other protected categories.

## Award classification (undergraduate Honours)
Source: brookes.ac.uk/regulations/completion/award-classification

| Classification | Overall average |
|---|---|
| First | 70%+ |
| 2:1 (Upper second) | 60-69.99% |
| 2:2 (Lower second) | 50-59.99% |
| Third | 40-49.99% |

- Weighting: Level 5 (Year 2) counts 25%, Level 6 (final year) counts 75% of the classification average (standard direct-entry students).
- The Level 6 component draws on 120 credits of graded modules.
- Marks are truncated (not rounded) to two decimal places - e.g., 59.6875% is recorded/used as 59.68%, which matters at classification boundaries.
- Students entering directly at Level 6 (e.g. with prior credit/foundation degree) are classified on their Level 6 average alone (100% weight).
- A separate Grade Point Average (GPA) system also exists in parallel (brookes.ac.uk/regulations/completion/grade-point-average) - Brookes reports both a classification and a GPA on the transcript; full GPA calculation detail wasn't captured from the page excerpt fetched - consult that page directly for a deeper dive.

## Exceptional (Extenuating) Circumstances
Sources: brookes.ac.uk/students/your-studies/exceptional-circumstances; .../application/how-to-apply; brookes.ac.uk/regulations/assessment-and-progression/exceptional-circumstances

### What normally counts (with evidence)
Death of a close relative/friend, short-term illness (flu, COVID, food poisoning), serious medical conditions/injuries, hospitalisation, being a victim of crime, major life disruption (divorce, burglary, assault), severe mental-health impact, pregnancy complications, asylum/immigration issues.

### What does NOT normally count
Weddings/travel plans, general exam stress or deadline pressure, computer failure or lost work, missed-deadline claims without underlying cause, planned events (house moves, holidays, celebrations), childbirth itself (as distinct from complications), sporting commitments.

### Process
- Assessment categories affected: (A) deadline-based coursework, (B) non-examined events like tests/presentations, (C) formal exams - outcomes can include deadline extensions, rescheduled assessment, or a further attempt without penalty.
- How to apply: via "My Requests" on the Student Information portal, categorised as Types A-D.
- Timing: apply as soon as circumstances arise and ideally before the deadline; for exam-day issues, within 48 hours of the assessment start.
- Evidence: normally required within 10 working days of the original claim (5 working days if the application itself was late). Claims without evidence, or without at least an explanation of what evidence is forthcoming, are unlikely to succeed unless a formal extension to provide evidence is separately agreed.
- Late applications: still possible but must explain the delay and provide evidence.
- Contacts: Student Support Coordinator or the Student Investigation and Resolution Team handle queries.
- Related support: Inclusive Support Plans and Counselling Service are signposted alongside the EC process for students with ongoing/underlying issues.
- An independent/plain-English companion resource: Oxford Brookes Students' Union advice page - brookesunion.com/advice/academic/ecs
